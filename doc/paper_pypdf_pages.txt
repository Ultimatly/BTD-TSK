Recognition of Sleep-Wake Stages by Deep
Takagi-Sugeno-Kang Fuzzy Classiﬁer
with Random Rule Heritage
Ta Zhou , Guanjin Wang , Kup-Sze Choi , Member , IEEE, and Shitong Wang
Abstract—Due to the existence of noise and/or uncertainty in
available sleep-wake stage data, recognition of sleep-wake stages
with an efﬁcient feature extraction has been facing the following
challenges: (1) how to handle these uncertainties; (2) how to have
interpretable recognition; (3) how to make recognition of sleep-
wake stages to have good testing performance (i.e., generalization
capability). To circumvent these three challenges, a deep Takagi-
Sugeno-Kang (TSK) fuzzy classiﬁer with random rule heritage
called Drrh-TSK-FC is proposed for recognition of sleep-wake
stages by integrating enhanced generalization capability of deep
structure in fully excavating sleep information hidden in sleep
stage data, together with the excellence of fuzzy classiﬁers in
handling uncertainty and providing interpretability in this study.
Drrh-TSK-FC assures both enhanced classiﬁcation performance
and strong interpretability by stacking the original input space
plus the randomly projected outputs of the previous TSK fuzzy
sub-classiﬁer into current sub-classiﬁer in a deep learning way.
Besides, it has three distinctive characteristics: (1) the cognitive
behavior is mimicked that people often leverage the past experi-
ences (i.e., rules) to solve new yet similar problems. (2) in order
to tackle with the changing importance of features caused by the
randomness in the generation of the input space for each TSK fuzzy
sub-classiﬁer, some randomly generated short fuzzy rules are used.
(3) a linear combination of all TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁers is taken to
facilitate with possibly different importance of the features in each
sub-classiﬁer . Moreover, Drrh-TSK-FC can be quickly trained by
our previous work—least learning machine (LLM) or its variant in
this study. The experimental results on recognition of sleep-wake
stage tasks indicate that except for strong interpretability, the pro-
posed Drrh-TSK-FC still keeps at least comparable classiﬁcation
performance to the adopted comparative methods.

I. I NTRODUCTION
S
LEEP can supplement human energy in time, enhance their
own resistance and immunity, and effectively promote the
normal growth and development of the human body. A healthy
sleep is also very important to protect people’s mental health
and normal psychological activities[1]. Therefore, the diagnosis
and treatment of sleep disorders has become one of the urgent
problems to be solved [1].
Recognition of sleep-wake stages is an important process in
the diagnosis of sleep-related diseases. Sleep stages are mainly
derived from polysomnography (PSG) measurements during
several hours of sleep [2]. PSG mainly records many physiolog-
ical signals such as electroencephalogram (EEG), electromyo-
gram (EMG), electrocardiogram (ECG) and electrooculogram
(EOG). Among the recognition of sleep-wake stages, EEG is
also one of the most widely studied physiological signals [2].I n
1968, Rechtschaffen and Kales developed a reading standard
(R&K) [3]. In general, most of the existing methods about
sleep-wake stage recognition ﬁrstly annotate time intervals in
the PSG for different sleep states, for example, six sleep states:
wake-up (W), stage 1, stage 2, stage 3, stage 4 and rapid eye
movement (REM) [4], [5]. Secondly, they generate the off-line
stage-wake data by using different readings of EEG signals
according to a R&K standard. Thirdly, they ﬁnish the recognition
task by carrying out various classiﬁcation techniques on the gen-
erated data. To date, typical methods include: (1) Salih Gunes’s
method [6] which integrated a feature weighted (KMCFW) data
preprocessing strategy together with K-means+KNN to ﬁnish
the corresponding recognition task; (2) C.A.Holzmann’s method
which simply takes an artiﬁcial neural network to recognize
sleep/wakefulness states in a minute-by-minute ofﬂine manner
[7]; (3) L.G.Doroshenkov’s method [8] which utilizes a hidden
Markov model (HMM) to provide a seemingly satisfactory
diagnostic and therapeutic program for sleepiness; (4) Y uan
Y e’s method [9] which uses a deep fusion attention network
to learn multi-channel bio-signals, quantify the importance of
each biomedical channel and implement enhanced feature rep-
resentations based on these channels [9]. More advances about
stage-awake recognition can be seen in [10], [11], [12].

Like the literature [13] and experimental evidences [13] in-
dicate, since the collected EEG signals and accordingly sleep-
wake stage data are more likely associated with noise and un-
certainties to a certain extent, an efﬁcient feature representation
(in both the preprocessing step and possibly successive classiﬁ-
cation steps) and robust classiﬁcation (especially having strong
uncertainty-handling and generalization capability) should be
simultaneously emphasized to handling sleep-wake stage efﬁ-
ciently. At present, although some existing works enjoyed their
efﬁcient preprocessing steps, they sometimes ignore an efﬁcient
feature representation during the classiﬁcation procedure, and
give almost no any effort for both robust classiﬁcation and
interpretability. In order to achieve robust and interpretable clas-
siﬁcation of sleep-wake stage data, as a preliminary attempt, we
have the following considerations in this study: (1) taking fuzzy
systems as a basic tool for sleep-wake stage data classiﬁcation
to share their strong uncertainty-handling capability and inter-
pretability [14], [15].B y interpretability in this study, we mean
that the IF-parts of fuzzy rules are linguistically interpretable
[14], [15]. (2) utilizing short fuzzy rules to reﬂect the importance
change of the features extracted after the preprocessing step and
hence have an efﬁcient feature representation during the classi-
ﬁcation procedure. (3) taking a deep stacked structure of fuzzy
systems to assure both satisfactory classiﬁcation performance
and enhanced generalization capability[16], [17]; (4) using short
rule heritage from previous fuzzy systems in the deep structure to
mimic the cognitive behavior that people often leverage the past
experiences (i.e., rules) to solve new yet similar problems. Based
on these four considerations, we develop a novel deep fuzzy
classiﬁer for sleep-wake stage data. The main contributions of
this study can be summarized as follows.
1) In order to solve a new problem effectively and quickly,
people generally consider how to inherit relevant knowl-
edge summarized from the experiences about solving
similar problems. Due to the similarity of the input space
between adjacent sub-classiﬁers in the proposed deep
structure, it becomes natural desire for us to mimic such a
cognitive behaviour in such a deep structure. In this study,
the concept of random rule heritage is proposed as the ﬁrst
attempt to achieve this goal.
2) The proposed deep structure shares the advantages of both
enhanced classiﬁcation performance and interpretability
of the state-of-the-art deep fuzzy structures [16], [17]
based on the stacked generalization principle [23].B e -
sides, it has its creativity in the generation of the input data
space at each layer (i.e., each TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁer
or each sub-classiﬁer for simplicity) [16], [17]. That is
to say, in order to propagate the outputs of the previous
sub-classiﬁer into the next sub-classiﬁer and simultane-
ously keep the diversity as much as possible between the
sub-classiﬁers, the input data space of each sub-classiﬁer
consists of the original data space plus the data space
generated from the outputs of both the previous sub-
classiﬁer and the randomly inherited rules. While deep
XAI (Explainable AI) including deep fuzzy XAI [48],
[49] has been becoming a hot topic in the computational
intelligence ﬁeld, owing to the use of random rule heritage,
such an interpretable deep structure as a more general
deep framework for fuzzy XAI obviously has its promising
potential for practical applications in the future.
3) Each TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁer of Drrh-TSK-FC is
equipped with some random short fuzzy rules so as to
facilitate with changing importance of features in the
proposed deep fuzzy classiﬁer. The use of random short
fuzzy rules actually simpliﬁes the proposed deep structure
and hence makes it become more interpretable.
4) In order to improve the classiﬁcation efﬁciency of the
proposed classiﬁer, the output of each TSK fuzzy sub-
classiﬁer is learnt quickly by our previous work–least
learning machine (LLM) [18], [19] or its variant in this
study. The experimental results on sleep-wake staging data
show that except for strong interpretability, the proposed
classiﬁer has at least comparable classiﬁcation perfor-
mance to the baseline methods.
The remaining parts of this study are organized as follows.
Section II brieﬂy reviews related works for sleep-wake stage
recognition. A novel deep changing-feature-importance-based
TSK fuzzy classiﬁer called Drrh-TSK-FC for recognition of
sleep-wake stages is described in Section III. Empirical results
of Drrh-TSK-FC are presented in Section IV. In Section V,w e
conclude this study.
II. R ELA TEDWORKS
Now we summarize the existing works about sleep-wake stage
recognition. Owing to today’s prevalence of recently developed
deep learning, here we call all the existing sleep-wake stage
recognition methods rather than deep learning methods the
traditional methods for easy understanding.
1) Traditional Recognition Methods for Sleep-Wake Stage
Since the early 1990s, although traditional automatic recog-
nition of sleep-wake stages is a draining, inefﬁcient and sub-
jective evaluation process, it has become one of the hottest
research topics. Such existing researches usually include the
following steps: (1) pre-processing such as noise removal from
original EEG signals; (2) extracting features; (3) developing an
appropriate classiﬁer to evaluate the acquired sleep-wake stage
dataset. Traditional recognition methods roughly deal with the
following technologies [1], [13], i.e., expert evaluation model
[20], extreme learning machine (ELM) [37], hidden Markov
models [21], random forest [10], [22], Linear Discriminant
Analysis (LAD) [24], subject-speciﬁc classiﬁcation model [25],
features segmentation and self-organization (clustering) [38]
and fast discriminative complex-valued convolutional neural
network (FDCCNN) [1]. However, these methods sometimes
do not yield desirable results for us. Besides, these existing
methods do not break the following barriers: (1) Noise existing in
current sleep monitoring systems makes it extremely difﬁcult to
extract their features accurately and even makes the importance
of extracted features become changing over time [39].A sa
result, the extracted features of sleep-wake stage data often fail
to clearly represent features [40]. (2) There exist uncertainties
in the sense of both the importance of each feature over time

2) Deep Learning Methods for Sleep-Wake Stage Recognition
In contrast to the above traditional methods, deep learning
methods for sleep-wake stage recognition are taken as the latest
efforts in breaking through one or some of the above barriers.
For example, Tuncer Seda Arslan[20] et al. developed a decision
support system to determine sleep disorders. They identiﬁed
sleep patients and healthy individuals only by pulse transit time
(PPT). Their proposed method yields good classiﬁcation results,
especially using the deep learning method to successfully cope
with the difﬁculty in an efﬁcient feature extraction[20]. Junming
Zhang [13] et al. proposed an automatic sleep stage method by
constructing several sparse deep belief nets to extract features
from sleep stage signal and hence improve the classiﬁcation
accuracy of sleep staging. Based on neurophysiological signals,
Y udong Sun [41] et al. designed a deep convolution neural
network model for automatic recognition of sleep-wake stages
by successively increasing the depth, extracting the multi-level
features and training the corresponding modules [41]. The de-
veloped model also yielded good classiﬁcation results in the
sense of the macro-averaging F1-score [41]. However, since
these models behaves in black-box way, and they do not consider
both the changing importance of the features and the uncertainty
existing in sleep-wake stage data, their improvements are still be-
ing expected. Previous works[16], [17] reveals that deep stacked
fuzzy classiﬁers can achieve good trade-off between promising
performance and interpretability for a variety of classiﬁcation
tasks. Therefore, different from these existing deep learning
methods, we will develop a novel deep stacked fuzzy classiﬁer
to classify sleep-wake stage data in this study.

B. On the Deep Structure of a TSK Fuzzy Classiﬁer
Drrh-TSK-FC is developed to design an ingenious deep
learning structure for enhancing classiﬁcation performance and
interpretability for recognition of sleep-wake stages. Now we
describe the structure of Drrh-TSK-FC and its learning algo-
rithm and prediction procedure.
Here we state the design methodology of the proposed deep
fuzzy classiﬁer Drrh-TSK-FC in detail. Owing to practical
demand of both interpretability and promising classiﬁcation
performance, we take the stacked structure [23] as the principal
part of our design. This is because each TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁer
has its high interpretability, and such a stacked structure indeed
assures both interpretability and enhanced classiﬁcation per-
formance, according to stacked generalization principle [23].
In the proposed structure of Drrh-TSK-FC, the input space of
each TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁer keeps the same as in [23]. That
is to say, the original input space plus the randomly projected
outputs of the previous TSK fuzzy sub-classiﬁer is presented as

are detailed as follows.
A. On Data Preparation
Here we describe how to generate sleep-wake stage data from
the original EEG signals downloaded from https://physionet.
org/physiobank/database/slpdb/.
1) Preprocessing: Due to the existence of noises and uncer-
tainties in sleep-wake stage data, an ideal classiﬁcation method-
ology on such data should be robust in the sense of noise
removal, uncertainty handling and generalization performance.
In order to effectively tackle with noises and uncertainties in
sleep-wake stage data, except for robust classiﬁcation methods,
a necessary preprocessing step should generally be taken to
have considerable removal of noises in the original signals.
Male signals (including ECG signals, blood pressure signals,
electroencephalogram signals, respiratory signals, etc.) aged
from 32 to 56 and weighing from 89 to 152 kg are used in this
study [34]. The frequency of these signals basically concentrates
between 0.5 and 30 Hz. In our experimental studies, we use a
bandpass ﬁlter to retain the effective frequency signal between
0.5 and 30 Hz and eliminate the high-frequency noise signal
with many interference[34]. Due to the existence of various high
frequency noises in the original EEG signal, the signal waveform
is disordered [34]. We use a Butterworth bandpass ﬁlter to ﬁlter
the EEG signal. For example, taking slp02a as an example, we
intercept and ﬁlter the ﬁrst 1000 sample points to ensure that
the signal frequency is concentrated within 0.5 ∼30 Hz and the
signal waveform is smooth. However, there is still noise in the
EEG signal. In this experiment, DB6 wavelet basis function[34]
is used to further denoise the EEG signal, and global threshold
is used to quantify the high frequency coefﬁcient, and the signal
and noise are separated. Please note, there may still be some
degree of uncertainties after preprocessing sleep-wake stage
data. As pointed out in [14], [15] and the introduction sec-
tion, fuzzy systems have strong uncertainty-handling capability,
and deep fuzzy structures have strong generalization capability.
Therefore, the proposed Drrh-TSK-FC naturally has a great
potential for robust classiﬁcation of sleep-wake stage data.
2) Feature Extraction and Generation of Sleep Stage Datasets
We use Gaussian kernel principal component analysis
(KPCA) to extract efﬁcient features from 7500 features acquired
after preprocessing [34]. According to the EEG signals ob-
tained by KPCA, following [1], [34], we randomly combine 18
polysomnography segments (i.e., slp01a, slp01b, slp02a, slp02b,
slp03, slp04, slp14, slp16, slp32, slp37, slp41, slp45, slp48,
slp59, slp60, slp61, slp66 and slp67x) corresponding to each
signal to randomly generate a dataset. In this way, we generate
four datasets of sleep-wake stages, i.e., Data-1, Data-2, Data-3
and Data-4 in Table I, for the diversity of experimental studies.
In addition, we follow the same arrangement as in [46], [47]
to separate a dataset into its training and testing data for fair
comparison.

[0.4,0.6),Ω4 =[ 0.6,0.8) andΩ5 =[ 0.8,1.0]. Obviously, when
the clustering centers fall in these ﬁve intervals, the correspond-
ing fuzzy membership functions may be assigned with the cor-
responding linguistic values: not serious, little serious, medium
serious, more serious and especially serious, respectively.

V. C ONCLUSION
Due to the existence of noise and uncertainty in sleep-wake
stage, this study develops a deep TSK fuzzy classiﬁer Drrh-
TSK-FC to achieve an efﬁcient sleep stage recognition with
high interpretability. On one hand, Drrh-TSK-FC keeps the same
generation of input spaces in its sub-classiﬁers as in our previous
works such that it can well share the distinctive advantage of deep
stacked structures in the sense of enhanced performance and in-
terpretability. On the other hand, different from traditional deep
stacked structures, Drrh-TSK-FC considers the use of both short
fuzzy rules and random rule heritage in its each sub-classiﬁer so
as to reﬂect the importance changes of the features in adjacent
sub-classiﬁers and hence mimic human cognitive behavior in
leveraging the past experiences to address similar problems.
Besides, Drrh-TSK-FC takes a linear ensemble of all the sub-
classiﬁers to assure further improvement in its performance.
The experimental results show that Drrh-TSK-FC is superior
to other comparative classiﬁers for the tasks of sleep-wake stage
recognition.
There is a big room for the future study about this work
here. For example, how to determine an appropriate number
of randomly inherited rules or give a guideline about it for
various datasets is worthy for study in near future. While one
may argue that the importance changes of features should be
better expressed by their different weights rather than the use
of short fuzzy rules, how to generalize the use of short fuzzy
rules is another interesting topic. At present, our study only
concentrates on a linear combination of all the sub-classiﬁers
in Drrh-TSK-FC. Let us recall that another two commonly
used ensemble strategies, i.e., averaging and majority voting,
generally require much less computational burden. Therefore,
which ensemble strategy should be taken by Drrh-TSK-FC so
as to achieve good trade-off between classiﬁcation performance
and computational burden should be empirically studied for
various datasets. In near future, we will go on our studies from
these perspectives.
